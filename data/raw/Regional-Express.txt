Regional-Express

Regional-Express, abbreviated RE, is a category of regional passenger train service in Germany that runs faster and with fewer stops than the more local Regionalbahn (RB) trains, while still being classified below long-distance services like the InterCity and ICE. Regional-Express trains typically connect mid-sized cities and link smaller towns to larger regional hubs, serving as a backbone for daily commuting across many parts of the country.

Unlike long-distance services, which are priced individually per journey and operated almost exclusively by Deutsche Bahn, Regional-Express services are commissioned through competitive tenders run by regional transport authorities on behalf of Germany's federal states, meaning the operating company on any given RE line may be DB Regio or one of several private competitors such as Transdev or Netinera, depending on which company won that route's service contract.

Fares on Regional-Express trains are generally covered by regional transport association tickets and, since its introduction in 2023, by the nationwide Deutschlandticket subscription, which makes RE trains—along with RB, S-Bahn, U-Bahn, tram, and most local bus services—accessible without a separate ticket purchase for subscribers, a significant simplification compared to the more complex per-route pricing used for long-distance ICE and IC trains.

Rolling stock used on Regional-Express lines varies considerably by region and operating company, ranging from older double-decker coaches hauled by locomotives to newer electric and diesel multiple units, with double-decker trains particularly common on busier corridors due to their higher seating capacity per train length, which helps accommodate commuter demand during peak hours.
