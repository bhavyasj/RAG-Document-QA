Deutsche Bahn

Deutsche Bahn AG, commonly abbreviated as DB, is the principal railway operator of Germany, formed in 1994 through the merger of the former West German Deutsche Bundesbahn and the East German Deutsche Reichsbahn following German reunification. The company is structured as a joint-stock corporation, but its shares are wholly owned by the German federal government, making it a state-owned enterprise in practice even though it operates under private commercial law.

DB's operations span passenger transport, freight logistics, and infrastructure management. The passenger division runs long-distance services under the InterCity Express (ICE) and InterCity (IC) brands, as well as regional trains under the Regional-Express and Regionalbahn labels, and operates several metropolitan S-Bahn networks including those in Berlin, Hamburg, Munich, and the Rhine-Main area. The freight arm, DB Cargo, is one of the largest rail freight operators in Europe, moving goods across an extensive network that connects German industrial centers to ports and neighboring countries.

Headquartered in Berlin, Deutsche Bahn employs several hundred thousand people and manages one of the densest rail networks in the world, with tens of thousands of kilometers of track. The company has faced sustained criticism over punctuality. Independent reporting and DB's own published statistics have repeatedly shown long-distance trains arriving on time only a little over 60 percent of the time in various recent years, a figure well below DB's own internal targets and a recurring subject of public debate in Germany.

To address aging infrastructure, the German government and DB launched a large-scale renovation program in the 2020s, often referred to as the "Generalsanierung," which involves taking major rail corridors fully out of service for months at a time to overhaul tracks, signaling, and stations in a concentrated effort rather than through piecemeal nighttime maintenance. The first corridor to undergo this full closure and rebuild was the Frankfurt-to-Mannheim line, completed in 2024, with additional corridors following in subsequent years.

DB also operates an extensive ticketing and fare system. The Deutschlandticket, introduced in 2023 as a successor to a pandemic-era discount scheme, allows unlimited travel on regional and local public transport throughout Germany for a flat monthly subscription price, though it explicitly excludes long-distance ICE and IC services, which require separate tickets typically priced by route and booking time.
